# The F-35 Lightning II — B and C

*[Version française](F35.fr.md)* — *up to date on 2026-10-18*

A fighter flown over the real Earth, solo or in a room: the F-35B (short take-off, vertical landing) and the
F-35C (catapult and wires), the real aircraft's control law and protections, an always-on radar, the gun's lead,
missiles and fights, formations with AI wingmen and orders, three carriers at sea, ILS and carrier approaches, a
flight school where TARS flies first, missions and views of exception, a replay with its trailer.

- The plans: [PLAN-F35.md](PLAN-F35.md) (the aircraft, F1–F11) and [PLAN-AVIATION-AAA.md](PLAN-AVIATION-AAA.md)
  (the real F-35, combat, the flight, the living sky and sea, the carriers, missions, assistance, TARS, the
  views — phases V, C, F, T, M, P, A, I, S).
- The progress sheets: `docs/progress/f35/` and [`docs/progress/aviation/`](progress/aviation/) (one per step,
  French, with their pictures and measurements).
- What TARS knows of it: the knowledge base [`docs/aviation/en/`](aviation/en/f35.md) (eleven pages: the F-35,
  basics, energy, BFM, formation, carrier, ILS, emergencies, radar, weapons, traffic).
- Playing together: [NETWORK.md](NETWORK.md); controllers and HOTAS: [HOTAS.md](HOTAS.md); the helmet's and the
  HUD's symbology: [HUD.md](HUD.md).

figures below are given as the game measures them.

## Getting in

- **Fly the F-35** (the radial wheel, Tab held, when not flying) or **Prepare a flight** (flying a plane:
  Tab held › Navigation › Prepare a flight): an airfield, a start (lined up on the runway in use, on a 15 km
  final 900 m up, or cruising 40 km out at 6 000 m), the airfield's own hour, the weather, the fuel, a flight
  plan to another airfield — **Fly**.
- **The variant**: Settings › Game › F-35 › Variant — **F-35B** (the default) or **F-35C**. Changing it refills
  the tanks.
- **The flight school**, **Challenges, fights, missions** and the **Views of exception**: Tab held › Lessons &
  challenges (off flight: the wheel's *F-35 challenges*).
- Choosing the F-35 anywhere else puts it on the nearest runway; a craft left on a runway stays there (parked:
  the next one lines up clear of it).

### The radial wheel (Tab held)

Up to 800 px across (44 % of the view's smaller side), each label fitted to its sector, the centre saying the
item pointed at, its key and whether it is engaged; a quick Tab stays the next lock even when a slow frame let the
wheel open. Ten sectors:

- **Autopilot ›** altitude & heading ('), autothrottle (⇧'), NAV (⇧M), auto take-off (U)
- **Afterburner** (⇧W) · **STOVL** (⇧B)
- **Gear, flaps ›** gear, flaps, air brake
- **Weapons ›** (in a fight) next weapon, lock, decoys, end the fight
- **Radar ›** next lock, mode (RWS → TWS → STT), drop the lock, intercept (⇧Tab), formation on the lock
- **Flight ›** each room player's *wingman* place, leave the formation, AI wingmen (0–3), rejoin, formation ›,
  break left, break right, combat orders › (attack my target, engage hostiles, cover me), more orders ›
  (tighten, loosen, hold here, return to base, diamond pass)
- **Navigation ›** flight plan (⇧O), ILS approach, autopilot modes ›, map (M), mission map, prepare a flight
- **Lessons & challenges ›** flight school, challenges/fights/missions, views of exception
- **Camera ›** next view, photo, replay, trailer

## The aircraft

- **Model**: "F-35A Lightning II" by shangus930 (Sketchfab, CC-BY-4.0), rebuilt by `scripts/build-f35.py`
  (Blender): control surfaces carved and hinged, the gear and its doors, the canopy, the lift fan and its doors,
  the three-bearing swivel nozzle, the cockpit's stick, throttle and panoramic display; KTX2 maps
  (`scripts/build-f35-maps.py`). The F-35C is drawn with the B's model.
- **Engine** (`src/f35/engine.ts`): the F135 — 120 kN dry, 176 kN with the afterburner, its lapse with height
  (with the density above 11 km) and Mach, its spool, its fuel burn (rising with Mach).
- **The variants** (`src/f35/variant.ts`): the **B** — 14.65 t empty, 6 125 kg of fuel, a 42.7 m² wing of
  10.7 m span, the lift fan; the **C** — 15.8 t empty, 8 960 kg of fuel, a 62.1 m² wing of 13.1 m, a tailhook, no
  lift fan, a carrier's gear (8 m/s of sink at touchdown, 4.5 for the B).
- **Measured** against the published aircraft (`tests/f35-envelope.test.ts`, every figure within its band):
  Mach 1.60 at 11 km with the afterburner (1.07 dry), a ceiling of 54 500 ft, a sustained 4.7 g at Mach 0.8 and
  15 000 ft, a combat radius of 903 km on internal fuel, Mach 0.8 → 1.2 at 30 000 ft in 78 s, 7 g reached in 3 s,
  17.9°/s at the 230 m/s corner, 52° of incidence at full aft stick, a clean stall at 116 kt; take-off in 10.5 s.
- **STOVL** (`src/f35/stovl.ts`, `src/controller/stovl.ts`, the B): ⇧B converts in 6 s (the doors, the clutch);
  the nozzle comes down as it slows; below 65 m/s the hover law — the stick a speed over the ground, the throttle
  keys a vertical speed, let go: held over its point. The fan and the nozzle hold some 184 kN in a hover: about
  17 t.
- **Sound** (`src/audio/engine.ts`): the turbofan's whine and roar, the afterburner's crackle, the lift fan,
  dulled from the seat; the wing's buffet rumbling through the airframe at high incidence; the pilot's breathing
  in his mask from the cockpit (setting *Pilot's breathing*).
- **Effects**: the vapour sheet torn off the wing pulling hard, the wingtips' threads, the heat haze behind the
  nozzle, the view's shake.
- **The pilot's body** (`src/game/gstrain.ts`, setting *G effects*): the signed load drives it — the grey-out
  and the tunnel of the black-out above the tolerance, which a load held over 5 g wears down (recovered under
  3 g); the black-out held 1.5 s is a **G-LOC** — some 8 s unconscious, the hands off the controls while the law
  flies on, 4 s to come back; the red-out under −1.5 g.

## The control law and the assistance

- **The normal law** (Settings › Game › F-35 › Control law: *Normal*, the real F-35's): the stick asks a **load**
  — 1 g at rest, the limit at full aft (7 g, the *G limit* setting 5–9), −3 g at full forward — established in
  about half a second and held as the speed bleeds; the incidence bounded too (35° to mid-stick, 50° at full aft:
  the stricter of the two wins); the roll about the velocity vector, slower at high incidence; the rudder a
  "pedal turn" there; 4 g at most with the gear down. It never departs, whatever the stick does. Let go, it asks
  1 g, then holds the flight path once settled; past 60° of climb or 70° of bank, let go means no roll rate.
- **The direct law** (*Direct*, "DIRECT LAW" in amber on the HUD): the stick on the surfaces, no computer — the
  deep stall and the spin become possible (a sustained spin at 90°/s), out of it by letting go (about ten
  seconds); "Angle of attack" called past 30°.
- **The assistance** (Settings › Game › F-35 › Assistance):
  - **Manual**: the direct law.
  - **Realistic** (the default): the real F-35, its law and its limits.
  - **Assisted**: auto-throttle (the throttle keys set a speed, the afterburner apart), the incidence held to
    20°, the ground's protection always on.
  - **Beginner**: aim, the jet goes there — the stick moves a cyan ring in the view (roll turns it at 45°/s,
    pitch moves it 12°/s, ±25° of climb), the jet banks (60° at most) and pulls to follow it; auto-throttle, 20°.
- **⇧X**, at every level: the wings rolled level first, then the nose to a 6° climb at a safe 200 m/s (the
  afterburner if slow); stable 3 s, the altitude, heading and speed held by the autopilot. A firm stick cancels it.

### Auto-GCAS and Auto-ACAS (`src/game/gcas.ts`, `src/controller/gcas.ts`)

- **Auto-GCAS**: ten times a second the jet predicts the recovery it would fly now (0.5 s, the wings rolled level,
  a 5 g pull to a 20° climb) against the relief along its path over 25 s; when that recovery would no longer
  clear the ground by its margin (45 m + 0.12 s of speed), it takes over — the stick neutralised, the wings level,
  then a 25° climb (the afterburner if slow) — and gives it back once a level-off at the height reached also
  clears the next 12 s. On the HUD two chevrons close on the centre, then meet in red: "AUTO GCAS — FLY UP".
- **Auto-ACAS**: the others are the radar's (the fight's craft, the real traffic, the room's players, the
  wingmen). A conflict is a closest approach predicted under 150 m within 8 s, escaped vertically, away from
  the other (±15°, wings level), held until 300 m are predicted, then given back. **Since 2026-10-18 it counts only
  a craft closing on us, or one within 25 m**: players flying on our wing, a craft alongside at our speed, are left
  alone (it used to fly the leader away from his own wingman). Our own AI wingmen, a lesson's leader or drone, and
  a craft joined on purpose (an intercept's shadow, a tanker's pre-contact) are never conflicts.
- Both are on by default (setting *Auto-GCAS · Auto-ACAS*), forced on in the Assisted and Beginner levels; silent
  in the direct law, with the gear down, in a formation, an ILS or a deck approach, in STOVL, on the ground or under
  another autopilot.

## The autopilot

- **Holds**: ' altitude and heading (the stick flies through them, let go the new ones held), ⇧' the
  autothrottle, ⇧Z the afterburner, U the automatic take-off.
- **Flight plans** (⇧O; `src/game/flightplan.ts`, `src/ui/routepanel.ts`): the Earth's map, a click adds a
  waypoint (on an airfield: the airfield), its altitude and speed constraints, each leg's course, length and burn,
  the fuel on arrival, the bingo and its alert; reverse, direct-to; **Procedures** adds a departure point (6 km on
  the runway's axis, 900 m up) and the arrival's initial and final fixes. **NAV (⇧M)**: LNAV the legs on their
  great circles, VNAV the climb and a 3° descent from its top; the plan's end hands over to the ILS. Shown on the
  TSD and in the helmet (LNAV · VNAV, the waypoint and its caret on the heading tape); kept in the saves.
- **The autopilot's modes** (Tab › Navigation › Autopilot modes; TARS's `jet_mode`):
  - **Orbit here** (right or left): over a point, the radius asked or a 40° bank's at our speed (4 km at least),
    the altitude and speed held ("ORBIT").
  - **Hold here**: a racetrack over where we are.
  - **Terrain following** at 100, 150 or 300 m: the steepest climb a point of the ground ahead (14 s of flight)
    asks, 20° up and 12° down at most, the heading the autopilot's or NAV's; the stick's pitch suspends it while
    held ("TF 150").
  - **Air refuelling** (the next section): TEXACO 11 put 25 km ahead at 7 000 m on its racetrack (or close by, at
    our height), joined by the intercept behind its hose, then the probe flown into the basket.
  - **ILS approach**, **Fly to… (the map)**, **Aerobatics** (the challenges' demonstrations), **Modes off**.
- **Air refuelling** (`src/game/aar.ts`, `src/controller/aar.ts`, `scripts/build-tanker.ts`; Tab › Navigation ›
  Autopilot modes › Air refuelling; TARS's `jet_mode` `tanker`, `refuelMode`; the setting Game › F-35 › Air
  refuelling):
  - **The tanker**: TEXACO 11, an A330 MRTT's look — the A320's mesh painted a military grey, two hose-and-drogue
    pods under its wings (their pylons, their ram-air turbines) —, its hoses (24 m) and baskets trailed, the pod's
    lights red, amber, green. It flies a racetrack 40 by 20 km at 210 m/s, its turns at 25° of bank rolled into at
    3°/s (its pods 11 m out rise and fall with its bank), and calls them ("turning left", "rolling out").
  - **The probe**: the F-35B's and C's, out of its door on the right of the forward fuselage (2.5 s), its tip met
    with the basket's throat.
  - **The basket** hangs at its hose's end, wanders a few centimetres, and is pushed up and out by the receiver's bow
    wave in its last 5 m. Met within 0.42 m of its axis at 0.25–2.6 m/s, it locks; faster it bounces off ("too
    fast"), off-centre it is missed ("missed the basket"). Locked, the probe pushes the hose in (the reel takes it
    up): the fuel flows (20 kg/s) while it is pushed in 0.9–4.6 m; past 6.5 m the hose whips (**breakaway**, back
    15 m and cleared again); pulled back, a **disconnect**. Full (or a 2 000 kg practice offload with infinite
    fuel): back out, cleared to depart.
  - **The radio**: the tanker's own voice (subtitled TEXACO) — cleared contact, contact, fuel flowing, transfer
    complete, disconnect, breakaway, cleared to depart —, shared with the room.
  - **Three ways to fly it**: **automatic** (the systems fly it all: observation, pre-contact 5 m behind the basket,
    the contact at a walking pace, the hose held 2.2 m in, back out); **assisted** (they hold the probe on the
    basket's line and bring it to pre-contact; the throttle is the closing speed — forward in, back out, let go
    held —, the stick nudges the line); **manual** (the pilot flies, the HUD helps). By default as the assistance
    (the beginner's automatic, else assisted). In automatic the throttle hands over to assisted, the stick hard
    over to manual.
  - **The HUD** ("AAR PRE-CONTACT / CLEARED / CONTACT / FUEL FLOW / BREAKAWAY / DEPART"): the basket's ring where it
    is (in the pod light's colour), the probe's tip, the line between them, the way to steer (▲▼ ◀▶ in metres), the
    gap and the closing speed (red past the bounce), locked the hose's range (its fuel's band, its whip), the three
    lights, the fuel given.
  - **The laws** (the last 15 m): across, the formation's at 0.55 rad/s; up, its own at 0.4/s without its integrators
    (some 60° of phase margin over the plane law's flight path, its 1 rad/s response); along, the throttle itself
    on the speed over the ground (a PI — the autothrottle, on the air's speed, chased every gust), 1.2 m/s at most
    the last metres, no air brake; the tip's residual trimmed slowly. Calm or light turbulence: the contact first
    time; moderate: hard (as it is).
- **The ILS** (`src/game/ils.ts`, `src/controller/ils.ts`; Tab › Navigation › ILS approach, TARS's `approach`):
  the localizer and a 3° glideslope read as a receiver does; to the initial fix 18.5 km out (a racetrack hold
  there if asked), the localizer intercepted, the glideslope captured, the gear and flaps at the final fix
  (9.3 km), the minimums at 60 m — unstable by more than a dot, a **go-around** to 600 m and round again —, the
  flare from 14 m, the braked roll and a grade out of 20. Already established on final it carries on. The needles
  on the HUD (magenta diamonds), the runway, its distance and the mode ("ILS 22 KEDW … AP LOC GS"). The **visual
  circuit** (the flight school's): downwind 300 m over the field, 1.8 km abeam; the **base** turn begun when a 40°
  bank's radius rolls out on the centreline, then the **final** on the 3° path — the approach's autopilot flies
  both.
- **Intercept** (⇧Tab, or Tab › Radar › Intercept): below.
- The stick, the throttle or another autopilot take a mode off; the altitude, heading and speed of the moment
  are held.

## The radar (`src/game/radar.ts`, `src/controller/radar.ts`)

- **Always on** in the air, in a fight or not: the AN/APG-81's beam sweeps ±60° ahead in 2 s (±35° in
  elevation); a contact is measured as the beam crosses it; its range with its size (150 km on a 5 m² fighter,
  ~95 km on 0.8 m²); the radar horizon (4/3 of the Earth's radius), the relief masking the line of sight, the
  Doppler notch looking down; tracks kept between sweeps, dropped after 5 s unmeasured.
- **Modes** (Tab › Radar › Mode): RWS (contacts), TWS (tracks with their speeds and courses), STT (the lock alone,
  measured each frame, lost out of the cone).
- **IFF**: a friendly or neutral transponder answers within 120 km; a silent contact stays *unknown* until seen
  within 3 km ahead; a mission's targets known hostile. Friendly blue, neutral white, unknown yellow, hostile red.
  The **datalink**: friends always known, drawn as hollow circles, not lockable (except in the free fight).
- **Tab** locks the nearest track, then the next. **A Tab before any contact** (just after a match's start, the
  beam's first sweep not done) no longer says "Lock dropped": "Radar: searching — the first contact locked" —
  the beam swept at once and the first track locked within 3 s, said when it is.
- **On the HUD**: the scope (bottom left, heading up) with the relief under it — red above our height, amber
  within 300 m below, green lower, the sea dark blue (setting *Relief on the radar*) —, the contacts' chevrons
  with their tracks and heights, the missiles in flight, the lock ringed and named, the range rings (setting
  *Radar range*: auto, 10–160 km); out of a fight a lock box on the target (its name, distance, speed, altitude,
  an arrow at the edge when off screen). On the PCD's TSD: NATO symbols, tracks, the lock's ring, the datalink
  dashed.
- Its sources: the fight's craft, the room's players, the real traffic and ships, the wingmen, the carriers and
  their escorts.

## Intercept and the gun's lead

- **The cues** (`src/game/intercept.ts`) on a lock: the collision heading ("INTC 070") and its steering cue, the
  time to intercept (TTI), the aspect angle ("AA 35R"), the pursuit flown (LEAD, PURE, LAG with its degrees), the
  heading crossing angle (HCA).
- **INTERCEPT** (⇧Tab; `src/controller/intercept.ts`): a stern conversion — a point offset by the room to turn,
  the turn begun when a 45° bank would end 2 km behind —, then within 3 km of the slot the formation law: **300 m
  behind, 60 m on its left**, level ("ICPT", "JOIN", "SHDW"); in place, the radar in STT on it, identified by
  sight, "In formation on …" from TARS. A target turning (a drone in orbit) is chased onto its slot; out of the
  beam it is followed by the shared picture (the AWACS, its ADS-B, the datalink) or dead-reckoned 2½ min at most.
- **Formation on the lock** (Tab › Radar): on a locked airliner, on its wing at the formation and place chosen.
- **The gun sight** (`src/game/gunsight.ts`): the target's future place from its velocity **and its acceleration**
  (its turn), the time of flight and the aim solved together with the fight's own ballistics; the pipper where
  to shoot, a range bar (a full turn for 2 km, a mark at 1.2 km), **IN RANGE** from 150 m to 1.2 km, red "GUNS"
  when the cross is on it in range; the **funnel** (where the rounds fired along the gun's axis in the last 1.5 s
  are now, a target's wingspan wide) with the trigger held or the target under 1.5 km; one round in five a tracer.
  At the bench, on a fighter turning at 5 g: 100 %, 100 %, 86 % of hits at 400, 700, 1 000 m.

## Weapons and fights

- **Weapons**: the 25 mm gun (220 rounds), the AIM-120C (radar, beyond 15 km) and the AIM-9X (infrared, off the
  nose) by proportional navigation, the GBU-32 JDAM glided to its point, the **JSM** (anti-ship: only on a locked
  ship, sea-skimming at 30 m and some 280 m/s, diving in its last 5 km, 160 km of range, a frigate sunk in one),
  flares and chaff. Loadouts: air-to-air, air-to-ground, gun, anti-ship (2 JSM, 2 AIM-120, 2 AIM-9X).
- **Threats**: aggressor fighters (patrol, engage with their missile and gun, defend with decoys; *Aggressors*:
  easy, normal, ace), SAM sites (SA-11, SA-6) that fire on what flies over 150 m in their reach, hostile frigates
  (six SAMs, 32 km: fly low and pass under).
- **The HUD**: target boxes, the lock with its range and closure, the launch zone and SHOOT in red, IN RANGE ·
  TURN in amber off the nose, the bomb's fall point, the missile warning and its bearings, the damage; the
  weapons' panel (right): the gun's rounds as a bar, each store a box, the one chosen framed, ∞ with infinite
  ammunition; a far craft of a few pixels marked by a dot in its box.
- **The shot**: a craft hit smokes (thicker the worse); destroyed it goes out of control — no lift, the nose down
  its fall, rolling, burning — and explodes once on the ground or the sea, a smoke column rising 45 s; a ship hit
  smokes, sunk it burns. **⇧L** (the pad's View held) locks the head on it to its crash; the **On target** view is
  an outside telephoto behind the jet.
- **Fights** (Challenges, fights, missions): drone hunt, guns only, two-ship intercept, ground strike — the
  Mojave's ranges. The aggressors and drones are drawn as F-35s (the only fighter mesh).
- **TARS calls the threats** (setting *Threat calls*), as a backseater: a missile at us on the clock with the
  answer ("Missile launch, 11 o'clock, 8 km — break left, flares"), again when close, its end; a hit taken. With
  the **radio roles** (setting *Radio roles*, on by default) the picture is **Darkstar**'s — the AWACS: new
  bandits by their BRAA, groups, SAM sites, kills, the bogey dope — (off: TARS calls it too); the wingmen speak as
  **Two/Three/Four**, the carrier as **Mother**, the LSO as **Paddles** — each its voice, its name and colour in
  the subtitles, shared with the room ("Ana — Roger ball.").
- **The free fight in a room** (setting *Free fight in a room*, Network group; in F-35s, out of a match, mission,
  lesson or challenge of one's own): weapons live, every craft a target — the other players, their AI wingmen and
  ours, the real airliners, ships and carriers the radar sees (taken in as stand-ins placed from the radar's
  picture) —, friendly fire on, the radar weapons free (a friendly track locks); shot down, back in the air 6 s
  later. The room's matches (everyone for himself, teams, co-op against the aggressors, races, landing contests):
  [NETWORK.md](NETWORK.md).

## Formations, wingmen and orders

- **Formations** (`src/game/formations.ts`; Settings › Game › Formation): echelon right and left, fingertip, vic,
  diamond, line abreast, trail, combat spread; tight, normal or wide; our place (#2, #3, #4) when on another's
  wing. A place changed slides to the new one, under and behind the leader to cross to his other side; in the
  tactical formations the wingmen cross over in a turn past 60°.
- **AI wingmen** (`src/game/aijet.ts`, `src/controller/wingmen.ts`): one to three — Two, Three, Four — flown with
  the F-35's own forces, in place as soon as we fly ("Two, in place"); a take-off as a flight (Two beside us, the
  element 200 m behind, 8 s later) and a landing as one (each its own approach to our touchdown). In a fight they
  chase their targets, fire AIM-120s (6–35 km) and AIM-9Xs, call contacts, "Fox three", "Splash"; they can be hit
  and shot down. In a room the host flies them, the others see them and have them on the datalink.
- **The leader's orders** (`src/game/orders.ts`; Tab held › Flight, or F13; TARS's `flight_lead`, and offline
  "Flight, go diamond", "Break left!", "Rejoin"…): rejoin, formation, tighten, loosen, attack my target, engage
  hostiles, cover me, hold here (a racetrack over the leader's point, stacked 300 m a place), return to base, break
  left, break right (90° at 75° of bank, the afterburner), diamond pass (the tight diamond, the fly-by camera
  ahead). Called on the radio, carried out by the AI wingmen and by the room's players flying in formation on us.
- **A player's wing**: **Tab held › Flight › "Ana's wingman"** (or TARS "join Ana"; or the room panel's
  *Formation*) — *Beside* first when far, then the formation flown for us in the **first free place** on his wing
  (his AI wingmen and the others already there counted: each page tells the room whose wing it flies); "FORM ANA"
  on the HUD; **Leave the formation** in the same wheel. The stick, the throttle or another autopilot let it go,
  the holds kept.
- **The real traffic** (Settings › Game › Traffic): real airliners (adsb.lol, OpenSky) drawn with their models,
  liveries, lights and contrails, shown a minute late on their reconstructed flight; real ships (aisstream) with
  their wakes. Neutral on the radar; interceptable, and flown in formation with.

## Carriers

The **Gerald R. Ford** (333 m), the **Kiev** (273 m) and the **HMS Implacable** (234 m) at sea
(`src/game/carrier.ts`, `src/controller/carriers.ts`): into the wind at 26–28 kt, pitching and rolling with the
sea, two escort frigates, friendly on the radar, the IFLOLS (the meatball) and the deck's lights at night; their
course a function of the clock, the same on every page of a room. Put at sea by TARS (his `carrier` tool: spawn,
land on, recover, take off, qual), by the missions, the flight school's Case I and the views of exception.

- **F-35B, vertical recovery** (`src/game/deckland.ts`; **F7** within 30 km): a gate 3.5 km behind on the port
  quarter at 150 m, up the port side slowing, the conversion, a hover alongside (22 m off, 18 m above the deck),
  cleared across over the landing spot (its lights amber, then green), down at 1.2 m/s to a pitching deck; set
  down, the throttle to idle, STOVL off, the brakes on. Too heavy to hover (above ~17 t) it says how much fuel to
  burn. Mother and Paddles on the radio; the grade: **OK** within 2.5 m and 2.5 m/s, **Fair** within 5 m and
  3.5 m/s, else **No grade**.
- **F-35B, short take-off** (**U** on the deck): converted braked, rolled at full power, rotated at 52 m/s (or
  45 m before the end of the clear deck), STOVL off past 100 m/s; some 150 m of deck at 20.9 t; vertical if the
  deck is too short and the jet light enough, refused otherwise (the Kiev's 150 m with full tanks).
- **F-35C, catapult** (**U** on the Ford): on the shuttle of one of its two bow catapults, 3 s of tension ("run it
  up"), the Shooter's launch to 72 m/s relative to the deck, an 8° climb held at the release.
- **F-35C, the wires** (**F7**): the axis intercepted, aligned on the moving angled deck, on the stabilised 3.5°
  glide; the hook catches one of the Ford's three wires (the target the 2-wire), stopped in some 100 m at full
  power; a **bolter** past the last one, full power and round again. **Paddles** calls every pass, the
  autopilot's or the pilot's once on final with the gear down: "Roger ball", "You're low", "Power!", the lineup,
  the wave-off, "Bolter, bolter, bolter!"; the grade: **OK underlined** 5, **OK** 4, **Fair** 3, **Bolter** 2.5,
  **No grade** 2, **Wave-off** 1, **Cut** 0.
- **Case I, II, III** (`src/game/trap.ts`), chosen by the hour and the weather (night — solar time before 06:30 or
  after 19:30 — or a low ceiling, rain, fog: III; clouds: II; else I) or forced through TARS:
  - **Case I**: the initial 3 NM astern at 800 ft and 350 kt, up the starboard side, the **break** past the bow (a
    level 60° turn), the downwind at 600 ft 1.2 NM abeam, the **180** begun past abeam the landing area and ended
    tangent to the axis, the groove and the ball.
  - **Case III**: the **marshal** 21 NM astern at 6 000 ft (racetrack laps as asked), the descent, configured at
    10 NM, level at 1 200 ft until the glideslope.
  - **Case II**: Case III's descent to 10 NM at 800 ft, then Case I's initial and break.
  - After a bolter, round again by the same way.
- **Qualifications** (`src/game/quals.ts`; the three *Carrier qualification* missions, TARS's `carrier qual`):
  day, night (23:00 local) and heavy sea (a 4.4 m swell, 9 m/s of wind); four passes graded by the LSO; qualified
  from a 3.0 average with no cut pass; the best kept by qualification and variant. The autopilot chains them
  (launch, circuit, approach, a hot pump between) or the pilot flies them.

## The flight school

Tab held › Lessons & challenges › Flight school (or TARS's `lesson`). **Eighteen lessons**: first flight, the
circuit, the landing, the ILS approach, Case I and the ball, formation flying, an intercept, guns, and the ten
aerobatic figures — each card its summary, an interactive diagram, its steps with their figures and the common
mistakes.

- **Watch the demonstration**: **TARS flies the whole lesson**, the pilot's part included, in his own words —
  the landing to its stop, the circuit's base and final, the guns to the kill. A demonstration is graded by a
  toast and its trace on the instructor's panel, **with no debrief window** (TARS does not correct himself); one
  that stops progressing for 90 s gives up and says so.
- **Guided: TARS, then you**: TARS flies the first part, saying each step, then "Your controls" — the holds let
  go (near another craft, the jet left trimmed until the stick and the throttle take it).
- **Try it**: the jet placed and the pilot flies, the instructor watching.
- **The instructor** (setting *Instructor*: off, calls, full): each deviation held a second past its limit
  called with its fix, the worst first, never two within 2.2 s, a lasting one repeated less and less often; the
  live panel (the manoeuvre's diagram and our trace, who flies, the steps, a gauge per parameter, **Explain**
  pausing on the lesson's card); the **debrief** — a letter and a grade out of 20, the charts of what was flown
  against the ideal, the phases, the two points to work on — and **Again**. A new lesson closes a debrief left
  open, starts unpaused, the last lesson's autopilots let go, the engine running.

## Setting down

Placed on a runway, the craft stands on the drawn runway's axis (WGS84), its nose down the end in use, level on
its gear — whatever the view (`tests/e2e/runway-placement.e2e.test.ts`). Set down anywhere else, it sits on the
plane through the ground under each of its legs: tilted with a hillside, every wheel on the ground
(`tests/e2e/ground-placement.e2e.test.ts`). On a deck it is carried by the moving ship, without drift.
