# Basic flying

## Attitude, power, trim
An aircraft flies by its attitude (where the nose points) and its power (the throttle). The nose sets the flight path; the power the speed — or, on the approach, the reverse: the power the descent, the nose the speed. The F-35's law holds the path once the stick is let go: a small input, a pause, read the result.

## Turning
Bank to turn: the bank tilts the lift, which turns the aircraft. At 60° of bank the load is 2 g; level turns need the pull and the power to hold the speed. The rate of turn grows with the load and shrinks with the speed: the tightest turn is at the corner speed (some 330–350 kt for the F-35), the fastest rate there too.

## Climbing and descending
Power climbs; the nose only trades speed for height. To climb at a constant speed: more power, the nose up until the speed holds. To descend: power back, the nose lower.

## The stall
The wing stalls past its critical incidence, whatever the speed. The F-35's law keeps the incidence bounded; in the direct law, unload (stick forward), power, and roll wings level only once flying.

## The instruments
The HUD: the flight path marker (where the aircraft goes), the pitch ladder, the speed (left) and the altitude (right), the heading tape, the load, the incidence (α). The vertical speed and the radar altitude near the ground.

## Common errors
- Chasing the instruments: small inputs, let them settle.
- Forgetting the power in a turn: the speed bleeds.
- Pulling the nose up to climb without power: the aircraft slows, then sinks.
