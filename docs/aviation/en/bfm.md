# Basic fighter manoeuvres (the dogfight)

## The geometry
- The aspect: where the enemy sees us (tail on: 0°; head on: 180°).
- The angle off: between the two noses.
- The lead pursuit points the nose ahead of him (a gun shot, closing); the pure pursuit at him; the lag pursuit behind (closing slowly, keeping the energy and the angles).

## The turn circle
Each fighter turns on its circle. Turning inside his circle (a tighter turn) gains angles; a wider faster turn gains energy. Two-circle fights (meeting head-on and turning the same way) favour the better rate; one-circle (turning the opposite way) the smaller radius.

## The moves
- The break turn: a maximum-load turn into a threat behind — to spoil his shot.
- The high yo-yo: up out of the plane to avoid overshooting, then down onto his tail.
- The low yo-yo: down to cut the corner and gain closure.
- The scissors: a series of reversals, each trying to end behind the other — slow speed and incidence win.
- The F-35's high incidence: its nose can point at 50° of incidence for a shot — at the cost of all its speed.

## The weapons' envelopes
- AIM-9X: off-boresight, close (to some 14 km), a heat-seeker.
- AIM-120: beyond visual range (to some 55 km), radar-guided.
- The gun: 25 mm, some 220 rounds, inside 1 km, the lead computed by the HUD's funnel.

## The defence
A missile at us: the RWR's warning, then beam it (90° to its line), flares (heat-seeker) and a hard turn at the last moment.
