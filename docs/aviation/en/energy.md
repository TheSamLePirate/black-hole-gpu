# Energy

## Speed and height are one account
The total energy: the kinetic (the speed) and the potential (the height). Trading one for the other is free (a zoom climb, a dive); only the engine adds energy, and the drag takes it away. The specific excess power (Ps): what the engine gives beyond the drag, in m/s of climb at constant speed — high, the jet gains energy; negative, it loses some in every manoeuvre.

## The corner speed
The speed at which the load limit and the lift limit meet: the fastest turn rate and the tightest radius. For the F-35 some 330–350 kt (170–180 m/s). Faster, the turn is wider at the same load; slower, the wing cannot reach the load.

## Managing it in a fight
- High and fast is an asset: it can be traded for a turn, a climb, a shot.
- A sustained turn (no energy lost) is near 4.5 g at medium height for the F-35; beyond, every second of pull costs speed.
- Afterburner in the fight: it costs fuel (some three times the dry flow) but holds the energy.

## On the approach
The energy is managed the other way: on the glideslope, high means power back, low power on; fast means power back, slow power — the path and the speed together.

## Rules of thumb
- 1 g of extra load at the corner costs some 10 m/s every few seconds without the afterburner.
- A 1 000 m zoom from 250 m/s leaves some 200 m/s.
