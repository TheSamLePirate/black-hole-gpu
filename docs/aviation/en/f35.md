# The F-35 — systems, limits, procedures

## The aircraft
The F-35B (short take-off, vertical landing: a lift fan behind the cockpit, a swivelling nozzle) and the F-35C (the carrier's: a bigger wing, 62.1 m², a tailhook, no lift fan). One F135 engine: some 120 kN dry, 176 kN with the afterburner (the F-35B's F135-PW-600; the A's 191 kN). Internal fuel: 6.1 t for the B, 9 t for the C. Empty mass some 14.7 t (B), 15.8 t (C).

## The limits
- Load: +7 g (the setting can lower it), −3 g; 4 g at most with the gear down.
- Incidence: 50° at full aft stick with the real law — departure-proof; 20° in the assisted levels.
- Speed: Mach 1.6 at altitude; some 700 kt in the dense air low down. The ceiling: above 50 000 ft (15 km).
- The gear: 250 kt (130 m/s) to lower it.

## The control law
The real F-35's: the stick asks a load (1 g at rest, the limit at full aft), the incidence bounded; let go, the flight path and the bank are held. The direct law (manual level): the stick on the surfaces, no computer — stalls and spins possible.

## The keys that matter
- Throttle: Shift/Alt held (or the lever); W full, X cut; ⇧W the afterburner.
- ' the autopilot (altitude and heading held, the stick flies through it), ⇧' the autothrottle.
- G the gear, P the flaps; ⇧B STOVL (the B); ⇧M NAV (the flight plan), ⇧O its panel.
- ⇧X back to stable flight (wings level, a gentle climb, a safe speed).

## Its protections
The Auto-GCAS takes the jet at the last moment before the ground — wings level, a 5 g pull — and gives it back once clear. The Auto-ACAS escapes another aircraft on a collision course — wings level, a climb or a descent away from its side, kept until clear: a conflict is only a craft closing on us whose closest approach in the next 8 s falls within 150 m, or one within 25 m whatever its motion; two jets side by side at the same speed are none. Left alone: our AI wingmen, the room's players flying on our wing, an intercept's shadowed aircraft, a lesson's leader and drone. Both are on by default, always in the assisted levels, off in the direct law, with the gear down, in a formation or an approach.

## Normal procedures
- Take-off: full dry power or the afterburner, rotate at some 130 kt (67 m/s), gear up once climbing.
- Cruise: Mach 0.8–0.9 at 9–13 km: the best range.
- Landing: the approach speed some 140–160 kt, the gear and flaps at the final fix, 3° down.
