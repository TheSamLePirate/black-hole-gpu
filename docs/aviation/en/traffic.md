# The traffic

## The real aircraft
The real air traffic (ADS-B: adsb.lol, OpenSky) drawn where it is — some 1 minute late by default, smoothed on its flight laws —, with its callsign, registration, type and mesh (A320, CRJ900…). The real ships (AIS) at sea.

## Reading it
- On the radar: the airliners with their transponder (squawk), neutral.
- On the map: their arrows, their callsigns close in.
- TARS lists what is about (traffic tool): the callsign, the type, the distance, the bearing, the height, the heading.

## Intercepting one
The intercept (the radar's lock, or TARS: "intercept AFR123"): the run, the turn onto its track, the join, the shadow 300 m behind on its left — the identification at visual range, as an air-policing mission does.

## A mission's real flight
A mission can take over a real flight (the one named, else the nearest heard): it leaves the traffic drawn and flies on as the mission says — a deviation, its transponder off, an escort somewhere.

## Rules
Keep clear of airliners: the Auto-ACAS escapes a craft closing on a collision course (or within 25 m); in the game's missions, an airliner is neutral — never a target. Only a room's free fight takes the real airliners and ships the radar sees in as targets (the weapons page).
