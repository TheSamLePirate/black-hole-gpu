# Entry and landing

## Deorbit
A retrograde burn lowers the periapsis into the air. About the Earth, a periapsis of 40–60 km brings the craft down; too steep (under 20 km) the load and the heat soar, too shallow (above 80 km) it skips. The entry autopilot (⇧G) plans the deorbit burn for a site, flies the entry and the glide.

## The entry corridor
The heat shield first, nose high (the Ranger at about 40° of incidence). The plasma peaks near 70 km; the load near 3–4 g. The crew's view and the HUD show the heat flux and the load; past 6 g or 250 W/cm² the corridor is lost.

## The glide (Ranger)
Below 30 km the Ranger flies as a glider: energy managed with the bank (S-turns), the runway's approach on the heading alignment circle, the final at 300 m per 4 km of distance, the flare from 14 m. The gear down by 500 m (G).

## A powered landing (Lander)
On an airless body the engine brakes all the way: the descent orbit's periapsis 15 km over the site, the braking burn, then the approach phase pitching up to see the ground, the vertical descent at 1–3 m/s. F7: the landing autopilot; during its approach the landing point designator (the LPD) moves the point with W A S D. The DESCENT panel counts the hover seconds the propellant leaves: at 60 the call "sixty seconds", at 30 land or abort.

## Take-off
U: the take-off autopilot climbs to the launch goal's orbit (its height and inclination), vertical first then pitching over, and circularizes at the top.
