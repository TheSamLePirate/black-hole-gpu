# Manoeuvres

## Circularize
At the apoapsis, a prograde burn raises the periapsis until the orbit is round. Key 9 (or the CIRC button): the autopilot burns at the next apoapsis and stops at a circle (eccentricity under 0.003). From the ground, the take-off autopilot (U) flies the climb then circularizes at the launch goal's height.

## Hohmann transfer
To change a circular orbit's height: a prograde burn puts the apoapsis at the new height, half an orbit later a second burn circularizes there. It is the cheapest two-burn way between circles. From 400 to 800 km about the Earth: about 104 m/s then 103 m/s. The flight computer's manoeuvres (map M, ORBIT tab) plan it as two nodes; EXECUTE flies them.

## Plane change
A burn along normal or anti-normal at a node of the orbit, where it crosses the plane wanted. Its cost grows with the speed: 1° at 7.7 km/s costs 134 m/s — change planes high and slow when you can.

## Manoeuvre nodes
A node is a planned burn: its time and its Δv along prograde, normal and radial. The navball's blue marker shows where to point. The node autopilot points the craft, starts the burn half its duration early and stops when its Δv is given. Nodes can be moved on the crew's orbit view and on the map.

## Low thrust
A weak engine (the Endurance's) cannot give a burn's Δv at once: the transfer autopilot spirals out over many orbits, thrusting along the velocity. Its time is days; its Δv is larger than a Hohmann's.
