# Orbits

## An orbit
A craft in orbit is falling around its body: fast enough sideways that the ground curves away as fast as it falls. Its path is a conic about the body's centre: a circle, an ellipse (its lowest point the periapsis Pe, its highest the apoapsis Ap), or past the escape speed a hyperbola. In low Earth orbit (400 km) the speed is 7.66 km/s and a turn takes 92 minutes; about the Moon at 100 km, 1.63 km/s and 118 minutes.

## The directions
Prograde is along the velocity, retrograde against it; normal is perpendicular to the orbit's plane (the right hand's thumb when the fingers follow the motion), anti-normal opposite; radial out away from the body, radial in toward it. On the navball: prograde a yellow circle with a cross, retrograde a yellow circle with an X, normal and anti-normal magenta, radial cyan, the manoeuvre node blue. Keys: 1 prograde, 2 retrograde, 3 radial out, 4 radial in, 5 normal, 6 anti-normal, 7 to the target; T the SAS.

## What a burn does
A prograde burn raises the opposite side of the orbit: burned at the periapsis it raises the apoapsis, at the apoapsis the periapsis. A retrograde burn lowers it. A normal burn tilts the plane — cheapest where the orbit crosses the target plane (its nodes). A radial burn rotates the ellipse, a poor use of propellant.

## Spheres of influence
Each body rules a sphere where its pull dominates (the Moon's 66 000 km, the Earth's 924 000 km). The game's path is computed through them: leaving one, the conic is about the next body. The orbit's figures on the HUD (Pe, Ap, inclination, period) are about the body of the sphere the craft is in.

## Δv
Δv is the speed change the propellant can still give (the rocket equation: exhaust speed × ln of the mass ratio). It is the budget of a mission: low Earth orbit to the Moon's ~3.9 km/s with the landing, Earth to low orbit ~9.4 km/s with the losses (7.9 km/s at least). The flight computer and the crew's Δv view compare it with the plan's burns.
