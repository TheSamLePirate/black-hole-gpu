# Relativity near Gargantua

## Two clocks
Near the hole time runs slower: dτ/dt is the craft's clock rate against the far-away clock (the Earth's, the room's). In orbit about the Earth dτ/dt is 1 to nine decimals; on Miller's orbit (10 M from a nearly extremal Kerr hole) it is about 1/61 000 — an hour there is seven years on Earth.

## What the game shows
The HUD near Gargantua: dτ/dt and the ship's own clock; the cockpit's CLOCKS screen: the ship's τ, and EARTH + the time gone by on the Earth beyond it; the crew's Relativity view: dτ/dt, one hour aboard = … on Earth, β and γ, the room's clock against the ship's. The crew's ground stations lose the live link behind the wormhole: only recorded messages, delivered when it comes back.

## The hole's radii
The horizon (r₊ = M(1 + √(1 − a²))), the ergosphere (2 M in the equator: nothing can stand still there), the prograde photon orbit and the ISCO (the innermost stable circular orbit — about 1.2 M for a spin of 0.999). Inside the ISCO no circular orbit holds: a craft spirals in.

## Flying there
Orbits are geodesics of Kerr: the map draws them in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates. Burns are measured by the ZAMO (the observer turning with the space). Every hour spent low costs years of the Earth's time: plan the burns, warp out, and keep an eye on EARTH +.
