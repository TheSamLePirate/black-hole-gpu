# Rendezvous and docking

## Rendezvous
To meet a station, match its orbit at the same time and place. In practice: first the same plane (a plane change at a node), then a phasing orbit lower to catch up (a lower orbit is faster) or higher to wait, then the transfer that arrives beside it. The B key beyond 3 km: the autopilot plans the whole rendezvous, flies it, and docks; refused if the plan would cost more than 10 km/s (a date far from the station's known orbit).

## The approach
Inside a few kilometres the motion is relative: thrusting toward the target makes you climb and fall behind (orbital mechanics up close). Close slowly: under 1 % of the distance per second, never more than 0.5 m/s inside 200 m.

## The waypoints
The docking autopilot holds two waypoints on the port's axis: 220 m (30 s), then 20 m (20 s), before the final approach. The station-keeping autopilot (⇧8) holds a point fixed relative to the target (in its local frame when it turns, LVLH).

## Docking by hand
Six degrees of freedom: ⇧9 switches the RCS to translation (W S up and down, A D left and right, ⇧ and Alt forward and back). Align the axes first (the COAS reticle: a cross on the port's axis, the target port a circle — green under 1°), cancel the drift across, then close at 0.1 to 0.2 m/s. The capture needs: closing under 0.3 m/s, within 0.3 m of the axis, the axes within 10°, the turns apart under 3°/s.

## A turning station
The Endurance turns about its axis (its ring, up to 10 rpm). To dock to its hub, turn with it first (match its spin, B does it), then approach along the axis.
